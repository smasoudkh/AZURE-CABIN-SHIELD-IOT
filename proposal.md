# Technical Proposal: Mitigating Lithium-Ion Battery Thermal Runaway in Commercial Flight Operations

## 1. Executive Summary
The proliferation of personal electronic devices (PEDs) and high-capacity power banks poses a significant safety hazard in modern commercial aviation. Lithium-ion battery failures leading to Thermal Runaway inside passenger cabins can cause rapid fire propagation and toxic gas emission. This proposal outlines a comprehensive, data-driven framework to minimize, detect, and mitigate thermal runaway risks in overhead stowage bins, utilizing hardware prevention, software analytics, and strict cabin safety protocols.
## 2. The Three-Layer Defense Strategy

### Layer 1: Ground & Gate Prevention (Risk Reduction)
- Visual and Physical Ingestion Checks: Implementing mandatory gate-screening guidelines to identify compromised PEDs. Power banks showing physical bloating, cracked casings, or abnormal heat dissipation during boarding are prohibited from cabin entry.
- State of Charge (SoC) Restrictions: Aligning with ICAO recommendations to advise passengers against carrying fully charged high-output power banks. A lower SoC (under 30%) significantly reduces the total thermal energy available during a cell failure, limiting propagation.

### Layer 2: Early Detection Pipeline (The Cabin Shield Software)
- Continuous Thermal Telemetry: Deploying high-frequency temperature and rate-of-rise sensors integrated directly into the upper structure of Overhead Stowage Bins.
- Derivative Rate-of-Rise Algorithm: Utilizing the Python-engineered data pipeline to monitor sensor streams. The system alerts crews when it detects a thermal derivative threshold change equal to or exceeding 2.0°C per second, or an absolute temp threshold of 65.0°C, catching volatile cells before visual smoke develops.
- Connected Flight Operations: Integrating the diagnostic output directly with the aircraft's crew alerting systems, sending instantaneous location data (e.g., Bin 12C) to the Purser's E-Tablet and airline Operations Control (AOC).
### Layer 3: Active In-Flight Mitigation & Containment
- Advanced Lithium Battery Containment Bags (TCB): Equipping flight attendants with certified high-temperature polymer containment bags capable of withstanding continuous temperatures up to 1000°C and filtering out dense particulate smoke.
- Aggressive Direct Cooling Protocol: Standardizing response training in compliance with FAA and EASA safety alerts. Initial mitigation focuses on flooding the affected device with water or non-alcoholic liquids to break the thermal chain reaction across adjacent cells, avoiding standalone dry chemical extinguishers which do not provide necessary cooling.

## 3. Operations & Maintenance Integration
The diagnostic data generated by the detection script is designed to output standardized payloads. These parameters easily port into current airline information tracking networks—such as KLM’s LiMiTS or core AMOS database platforms—to auto-log system events, compile safety analytics, and immediately flag affected bins for post-flight MRO inspections before the aircraft is released to service.
